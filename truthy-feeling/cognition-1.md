# How a Judgment Becomes a Feeling

*That Truthy Feeling · Cognitive Science I · College-level explanation of mechanisms and evidence*

## Start with successful evaluation

Suppose you see how much water a glass contains, understand the question being asked, and judge its fullness accurately. The assessment is useful because it tracks something about the glass. Begin with that successful case. The first question is what a physical evaluator must represent and do to make such an assessment available for thought and action.

CST proposes that an evaluator maintains a simplified model of its current assessment. This correspondence schema would connect the feeling that a claim is right with choices to report it, use it, seek more information or stop investigating. It concerns new judgments and ongoing inquiry, not just stored conclusions. Starting with success is an explanatory choice, not a claim that every human judgment is accurate.

The philosophical companion asks what the assessment concerns, including whether a claim can fit its subject to a degree. Interpretation and conditions such as time, scope and assumptions help fix the question without requiring separate kinds of reference worlds. This post first develops the proposed mechanism and its tests, then considers error and social and biological influences as a distinct late part of the account.

## Concepts need not be switches

A glass holding 250 milliliters contains exactly 200. Under a deliberately proportional interpretation of fullness, its degree is 0.8. Knowing that exactly differs from being 80 percent sure it reaches the brim. The degree estimated and uncertainty about that estimate are different things. Correctly representing an intermediate value is a successful judgment, not a compromise between accuracy and error.

Human concepts give us reasons to investigate more than an on/off label. Rosch and Mervis studied differences in category representativeness. Hampton develops graded membership while distinguishing it from typicality.[1,9] An unusual bird does not become partly a bird merely because it is unusual. The challenge is to identify which relationships admit degrees and what those degrees mean.

Distributed models describe semantic knowledge through patterns across learned connections. Related work has been compared with human impairment on semantic tasks.[2,10] This supplies a possible implementation of graded, context-sensitive information, not proof that a given amount of neural activity is a truth degree.

CST proposes that a pattern can encode how fully a situation satisfies an interpreted claim, and that a schema preserves selected features for further use. The pattern need not occupy a single neuron or numerically equal its represented value. A rival could represent the same amount and apply a binary standard to the sentence. Numerical resemblance alone does not decide between those accounts.

## Combining concepts is the harder test

A system handling FULL and CLEAR separately still needs to understand “full and clear.” Taking the smaller membership degree is one formal choice for a conjunction, not an established universal law of human concepts.

Hampton found conceptual-combination patterns not captured by simple combinations of separately assessed memberships.[3] Context and relationships matter. A graded account may need richer structure than a scalar for each word. Intermediate ratings alone are insufficient: uncertainty, thresholds and response habits can produce them too. The stronger test concerns transformations, combinations and transfer to unfamiliar cases.

## A model can help direct the process it describes

The architecture has a longer history than CST. Nelson and Narens distinguish a cognitive process from a meta-level model that monitors and helps control it.[15] Mangan connects felt rightness with contextual fit, retrieval and feedback during thought.[14] These are close predecessors, not merely earlier references to confidence. CST's possible contribution is a more explicit account of what an evaluation represents and how its conditions guide the next inquiry.

Graziano and Webb's Attention Schema Theory adds a specific analogy: a simplified model of attention participates in its control.[4] Salient events draw attention from below while goals guide it from above. The model informs regulation rather than acting as a third independent force or a little observer.

Suppose you read a forecast while planning a walk, realize it describes a distant town, and look for the local forecast instead. You may have attended perfectly to the first report. Evaluation concerns whether it answers the local question. Recognizing the mismatch and selecting the relevant next check is a success of evaluation, not a failure merely because the first source was unsuitable.

The proposed loop runs from information to evaluation, from the represented assessment to inquiry, and back through what inquiry supplies. Costs and purposes influence checking without becoming evidence about the weather. The schema must add more than a confidence score: equally favorable answers may retain different conditions and therefore direct different next steps. An ordinary memory or metacognitive mechanism may already perform that work.

## A feeling and the action it predicts

Thompson and colleagues found that initial feelings of rightness predicted subsequent reconsideration.[16] Desender and colleagues linked confidence to additional information seeking while separating confidence from objective accuracy.[18] These findings give the general monitoring-and-inquiry relationship an experimental basis.

But Wang and Thompson manipulated rightness ratings without producing the corresponding change in choices to reconsider.[17] That matters. A rating is not automatically the controller of the behavior it usually predicts. It might share an input with the behavior, or be influenced by a cue that does not change the decision. A model must explain the association and the dissociation rather than selecting only favorable findings.

We can be confident that a binary claim is true or false, or confident about a specified intermediate estimate. The last case needs an explicit accuracy criterion rather than a borrowed yes-or-no confidence scale. Different questions do not, by themselves, prove that the brain uses independent mechanisms for their answers.

## How would we test a graded assessment?

A glass task could vary filling separately from the quality of the view. Clear intermediate filling can be compared with uncertain information about filling and with uncertainty about a crisp brim-full condition. Specify the question rather than naming every intermediate answer partial truth.

Check that people understand the rule, and test transfer to new containers and combinations. A taught membership rule and an ordinary-language use of “full” must not be pooled as though they necessarily mean the same thing. A related sequence using “nearly full” could compare degrees, categorical judgments and requests for a better view. Stable yes-or-no answers need not imply identical internal values. Thresholds, noise and changing interpretation must remain viable alternatives.

## When another person disagrees

The same architecture suggests a practical question: can information about a judgment identify what a disagreement needs next? In “the system is reliable enough to deploy,” one speaker may mean uptime and another answer accuracy. They might instead differ over test results, or agree on the predicted errors and disagree about acceptable trade-offs. Clarification, evidence exchange and deliberation address different problems.

A diagnosis can contain several possibilities or admit insufficient evidence. Restoring a forgotten qualification is one intervention, not the explanation of every dispute. Clarification can also change the chosen question rather than update one fixed proposition. Communication research already studies targeted repair.[13]

Test whether independently elicited meanings, grounds and limitations improve the next step. Competing methods need the same information, because elicitation itself may help. Score comprehension, well-grounded judgments and faithful identification of the remaining difference, not agreement alone. A genuine conflict of values may remain. A useful classification would not by itself establish that the brain implements it as a schema.

## When the assessment stops tracking its grounds

Successful correspondence is not a guarantee attached to the feeling of correctness. Repetition can increase judged truth, and information from fictional stories can enter later general-knowledge answers.[5,11] Possessing relevant knowledge and using it in a particular judgment are not equivalent. Yet the mechanism is disputed: Shechter and Klauer's reanalysis and new experiments support reconsidering knowledge-first processing rather than treating fluency precedence as settled.[19] That does not make all repetition effects disappear.

Source-monitoring accounts already examine how remembered information is attributed to its origin.[6] A self-model is useful only if its specified contents explain something beyond these alternatives. Asking “how true does this feel?” does not reveal an internal state directly; interpretation, value, uncertainty and reporting habits can all affect the answer.

Perceptual experience can also diverge from the external event. Powers and colleagues induced reports of absent tones after audiovisual conditioning.[22] Their task concerns perception and its evidential basis, not a proof that an assessment schema failed. A false percept, a mistaken inference, uncertainty and a correctly represented partial condition need not share one mechanism. They are distinct cases the broader account must handle.

## Social purposes and biological mechanisms

A major further complication is that evaluation can serve persuasion, affiliation or commitment as well as accuracy. Schwardmann and van der Weele found that a persuasion opportunity increased privately elicited overconfidence.[7] That motivates investigating the private assessment rather than assuming every strategic effect stops at public wording. It is a separate question from how a graded condition is represented.

The causal interpretation needs care. Zhang and Rand found similar self-persuasion in persuasion and summary tasks, consistent with exposure to a position helping produce the belief shift.[20] Social tasks may alter belief without a uniquely persuasion-driven self-deception mechanism. A test needs to compare what people actually encounter and process, not only the incentives named in the instructions.

Social and biological influences need not compete. On the physicalist account, social learning operates through biological systems. The further evolutionary question is why those systems may be sensitive to benefits such as cooperation and credible commitment. Evolutionary models show conditions under which overconfidence can pay,[12] but this is not proof of the actual origin of a human assessment mechanism. Current biological implementation, social learning and historical selection are related explanations at different levels.

CST asks whether these conditions affect what seems settled and whether inquiry continues. Moral conviction is one candidate context, not simply high confidence or a universal refusal to qualify a claim. Nor is social influence necessarily a source of inaccuracy: Jordan and Kteily found reputational benefits to considering opposing evidence before punishment.[21] Commitment to an action and willingness to examine evidence can coexist. Different error costs may also favor a different action without altering belief about the facts.

A proposed test would hold interpretation and evidence fixed, control exposure and effort, and separately measure private estimates, public commitment and checking. A public-only effect would weaken the stronger private-assessment claim. Private reports still require validation; incentives do not make them transparent windows into experience. The prediction must be context-specific, not “higher stakes means more certainty.”

## The difference between a useful account and a new label

Metacognitive models already connect self-evaluation with further thought and search.[8] Some might implement the proposed schema rather than compete with it. The question is what information is represented, how it is used and what predictions follow—not simply what the theory calls it.

The project's mathematical checks have not uniquely identified CST. Some candidates make the same predictions as ordinary selective-access accounts. The new tasks remain proposals, with no language models used as substitute participants. Graded semantic satisfaction, a self-model and a useful diagnostic practice are separate commitments. Error and social modulation are further phenomena to explain, not reasons to define all cognition by its failures.

The philosophical and cognitive papers are paired because neither can do the other's work. Meaning determines what a representation should track; evidence tests whether the mechanism tracks and uses it. CST is an attributed integration of existing resources whose specific additions must earn support through those comparisons, including when the assessment being examined is that CST itself seems right.
