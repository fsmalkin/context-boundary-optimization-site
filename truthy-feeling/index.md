# That Truthy Feeling: Toward a Physicalist Ontology

Five essays by **Fred Smalkin, Jr.** on what makes a claim true and how a physical mind evaluates it.

Start with ordinary competence: understanding a claim, recognizing how well it fits its subject, and using that assessment to decide what to do or investigate next. The series develops Correspondence Schema Theory as a proposed connection among meaning, degrees of satisfaction, uncertainty, and the regulation of inquiry. It then considers the important ways error, social purposes, and biological processes complicate the account.

## Status of the work

These are working essays for public consideration, not a claim that a distinct correspondence schema has been empirically established. The account draws on existing work in graded semantics, contextual rightness, metacognition, and epistemic vigilance. The posts distinguish inherited foundations, proposed connections, numerical illustrations, and untested predictions.

The literature review is ongoing. In particular, the final methods of Shechter and Klauer's *Rethinking Knowledge's Impact on the Illusory Truth Effect* have not yet been fully reviewed; claims relying on it are limited accordingly. Posting the essays does not certify an exhaustive prior-work review, and later revisions may correct or refine the account.

The author directed the arguments and revisions. AI was used substantially for research assistance, drafting, and editing—not only proofreading. The essays are independently hosted here; they have not been accepted or published by LessWrong.
